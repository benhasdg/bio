Kitchen Renovation

This was the biggest home project I've taken on—a full gut renovation of our kitchen, including a set of custom wood countertops I built myself.

Before: where we started.

The project started with demo—stripping everything down to the studs to get at the electrical and plumbing before anything else could happen.

Rebuilding meant getting cabinets, countertops, lighting, and flooring to all come together in the right order—miss a step and you're redoing something later.

The countertops were the part I was most excited about—custom rubberwood slabs I built myself. Rubberwood is a sustainable hardwood that's often overlooked, but it machines well and holds up, and at around $200 for the material it was a fraction of the cost of stone or solid-surface. The slabs were 2.1 inches thick, so I had to rout a recessed pocket into the underside to let the undermount sink sit flush. I saved every offcut and used the smaller pieces to fill in the sink's accessory rails instead of throwing them out.

The routed underside, cut to fit the undermount sink.

Cabinets in, ready for the countertops.

Applying the food-safe finish: Real Milk Paint's Half & Half Tung Oil with Citrus Blend.

The finish mattered as much as the build. I used Real Milk Paint's Half & Half Tung Oil with a citrus blend—food-safe, soaks deep into the grain, and genuinely pleasant to work with; the citrus solvent smells a lot better than most wood finishes.

Every inch had a job—nothing wasted.

The details took the longest—outlets in the right spots, drawers that don't rattle, a backsplash that actually lines up—but they're the part that makes it feel finished.
